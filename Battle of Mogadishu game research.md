# Mapping Mogadishu's 17 hours for a shooter

The Battle of Mogadishu fits a tight, readable game space. Most of the fighting happened in an area about 1 to 1.5 km across. The target building, the Olympic Hotel and the first crash site (Super 6-1) sit within about 250-400 m of each other in the Bakara Market / "Black Sea" district. The second crash site (Super 6-4) lies roughly 0.6-1.6 km to the south; sources disagree on the exact distance. The Task Force Ranger hangar at the west end of the airport was about **3 miles** away, and the relief convoy started from the New Port on the coast ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172); [CMH](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/2181/Somaliaarmy.pdf)). The battle itself lasted about 17 hours. It opened with a planned 30-minute daylight raid at 15:32 on Oct 3. RPG-7s took down two MH-60s by about 16:40. About 90-99 men were then trapped around crash site 1 overnight. A multinational armored column of about 100 vehicles reached them at 01:55, and the survivors arrived at the Pakistani Stadium around 06:30 on Oct 4 ([Marion, AF History](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf); [Wikipedia Timeline](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Timeline_of_the_1993_Battle_of_Mogadishu)). Six earlier raids from late August through September give a natural campaign ramp. They began with a night raid that grabbed UN staff by mistake and ended with a helicopter takedown of a moving car, the day the SNA first fired RPGs in mass ([USSOCOM/USASOC history](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/International_Security_Affairs/07-A-2365_Task_Force_Ranger_Report_Operations_in_Somalia_1993.pdf)). For a semi-realistic open-world FPS, the record supports a dense downtown core map and a larger 5-6 km operational map. It also supports about 15 well-documented mission set pieces and a militia AI built around smoke signals, crowds and RPG ambushes.

## The whole fight fits inside a six-kilometer box

**The downtown core.** The Olympic Hotel is on Hawlwadig Road, and Wikipedia gives its position as 2°03′04.1″N 45°19′28.9″E. Bakara Market surrounds it and was Habr Gidr clan territory ([Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)). The target was a building **one block north of the hotel** ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)). A site that collects period photos describes it as L-shaped with a courtyard ([PMC Tactical](https://somalia.pmctactical.org/somalia-olympic-hotel.php)). Ranger chalks fast-roped onto the four corners of the target block to form a square of blocking positions, and the ground convoy waited beside or behind the hotel ([Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf); [MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)). Sources disagree on the hotel's height. The Air Force history calls it a "seven-story" building, while MWI groups it with a handful of four- and five-story buildings. A game can choose either and stay defensible.

**Crash site 1 (Super 6-1), the "northern" site.** Most sources put it three or four blocks from the target, but they disagree on the direction: about 300 yards east, three blocks east, four blocks northeast of the hotel, or four blocks north ([Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf); [MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172); [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/113311/we_didnt_leave_anybody_behind_10th_mountain_division_veterans_reflect_on_mogadishu_rescue_missi)). Converting Wikipedia's coordinates puts it about **245 m northeast** of the hotel, which suggests blocks of roughly 60-80 m. The helicopter lay on its side against a stone wall in a narrow alley lined with walls about 10 ft high. Nearby was an open intersection of about 45 m that a pararescueman crossed under fire. The defenders took over four houses on "Freedom Road" and held about 20 residents inside them ([Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf); [Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))). The alleys wound so badly that the lost convoy passed within **half a block of the wreck twice** without finding it ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)).

**Crash site 2 (Super 6-4), the "southern" site.** This location is the least certain. Distance estimates include "less than a mile south" ([CMH](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/2181/Somaliaarmy.pdf)), seven blocks south of the hotel ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)), about ten blocks south ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/113311/we_didnt_leave_anybody_behind_10th_mountain_division_veterans_reflect_on_mogadishu_rescue_missi)) and about a mile away in the crowded Wadigley neighborhood ([Bowden ch. 8](https://erenow.org/ww/black-hawk-down/8.php)). Wikipedia's coordinate works out to only about 610 m south of crash site 1. Bowden describes the site as quite different from the stone center: a small clearing among tin huts and rag-roofed shanties, with a single tree, a fence flattened by rotor wash, and footpaths leading in.

**The wider city.** The operational map is built around a few key points:

- **Airport.** Task Force Ranger flew from the west end of the airport, a flight of about four minutes. The airport sits lower than the city, so Somalis could watch every launch ([CMH](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/2181/Somaliaarmy.pdf); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)).
- **New Port.** It was "a few miles up the coast" from the base. The port is less than three miles from the hotel, but the convoy needed more than 2.5 hours to cover that distance ([Seattle Times / Bowden](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19980204/2732545/black-hawk-down----an-american-war-story----rescue-convoy-in-confusion----chaos-reigns-as-commanders-assemble-multinational-rescue-effort); [MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)).
- **National Street.** A large east-west avenue that served as the column's main road ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/113311/we_didnt_leave_anybody_behind_10th_mountain_division_veterans_reflect_on_mogadishu_rescue_missi)).
- **K-4 traffic circle.** It lies between the airport and the southern site. The 10th Mountain QRF was stopped there, more than a mile short of its goal ([CMH](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/2181/Somaliaarmy.pdf)).
- **Pakistani Stadium.** A soccer stadium in the northeast of the city, with triage on the field ([CMH](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/2181/Somaliaarmy.pdf); [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/113311/we_didnt_leave_anybody_behind_10th_mountain_division_veterans_reflect_on_mogadishu_rescue_missi)).
- **University compound.** The QRF was based here, next to the US Embassy on the "far side of the city" ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)).
- **Other named streets.** Via Lenin, 21 October Road and Armed Forces Street, plus the abandoned "Green Line" that split North from South Mogadishu ([Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)).

None of the sources gave exact coordinates for K-4, the stadium, the New Port or the university, and the Old Port does not appear in any of them.

## Six raids, then seventeen hours

**The run-up in August and September.** On June 5, SNA militia killed 24 Pakistani peacekeepers. Bombs on August 8 and around August 22 hit US Humvees, and after them Washington approved a Ranger/Delta task force under MG William Garrison ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article-amp/212007); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))). Most of the force arrived on August 28, and militia mortared its hangar at 19:27 that same night, injuring four. TFR flew six missions before October 3:

1. **August 30, 03:09: Lig Ligato house.** The raiders detained 8 or 9 people, who turned out to be UN employees. The press mocked the mission ([USSOCOM/USASOC](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/International_Security_Affairs/07-A-2365_Task_Force_Ranger_Report_Operations_in_Somalia_1993.pdf); [Day, ACSC](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/2172/SomaliaTFR.pdf)).
2. **September 6: Old Russian compound.**
3. **September 14: Jialiou house / police station.**
4. **September 17: Radio Mogadishu.**
5. **September 18: Osman Atto's garages.**
6. **September 21: capture of Atto himself.** Atto was the SNA's chief financier. A beacon hidden in his cane let helicopters track his car, and snipers stopped it by shooting the engine block ([Wikipedia, Gothic Serpent](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Operation_Gothic_Serpent)). That same day US forces first met **massed RPG fire** ([Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)).

The official history counts missions 2-5 as tactical successes but gives no further detail. Game writers will need to invent the specifics.

On **September 25** an RPG shot down Black Hawk "Courage 53" at about 100 ft near the New Port, killing three. It was a 101st Airborne aircraft flying for the UN mission, not a TFR aircraft. The two injured pilots hid in an alley, and one fired his pistol until he ran out of ammunition. After nearly two hours a local man guided a UAE APC to them. A 10th Mountain QRF fought its way to the wreck and back ([Coffee or Die](https://www.coffeeordie.com/rescue-of-courage-53)). I found no source linking "Radio Aden," "Pasta Factory" or "Digfer hospital" to a TFR raid. "Checkpoint Pasta" usually refers to a separate Italian battle with the SNA, so treat those names as unconfirmed. A September 15 MH-60 shootdown that appears in one ACSC paper is probably a mix-up with Courage 53.

**October 3-4.** The intelligence came in between about 13:00 and 13:50: two SNA lieutenants were to meet near the Olympic Hotel ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))). The codeword "Irene" launched **19 aircraft, 12 vehicles and about 160 men at 15:32** ([Wikipedia Timeline](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Timeline_of_the_1993_Battle_of_Mogadishu)). The times below follow the consensus of Marion, the Wikipedia Timeline, ASOMF and Migflug. Day's 1997 paper puts both shootdowns 10-20 minutes earlier and has Super 6-2 at 18:30, which conflicts with every other source.

The commonly cited US toll is **18 killed**. Wounded counts range from 73 to 97 depending on scope and cutoff date, and a mortar strike on the hangar on October 6 killed one more Delta operator ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [USSOCOM/USASOC](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/International_Security_Affairs/07-A-2365_Task_Force_Ranger_Report_Operations_in_Somalia_1993.pdf)). Somali deaths are deeply disputed. The Red Cross estimated about 200, Aidid claimed 315, Bowden gives about 500, and Robert Oakley put killed and wounded together at 1,500-2,000 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))).

## A light raiding force against a city that kept growing

**Task Force Ranger** had about **441 personnel**. Its main parts were C Squadron of Delta, B Company of the 3rd Ranger Battalion, 1st Battalion of the 160th SOAR, a small group of DEVGRU SEALs, and 11 airmen of the 24th Special Tactics Squadron (3 pararescuemen and 8 combat controllers) ([OSD TFR Report](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/International_Security_Affairs/07-A-2365_Task_Force_Ranger_Report_Operations_in_Somalia_1993.pdf); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)). Delta rode the outside benches of four MH-6s, four men per aircraft. About 140 Rangers, most around 19 years old, roped in by chalks of about 12, each with an M60 gunner and an assistant ([Bowden](https://archive.org/stream/black-hawk-down_201701/black-hawk-down_djvu.txt)). Delta carried the **Colt 723 CAR-15**, an M16A1-type carbine with a carry-handle-mounted Aimpoint red dot ([Military Times](https://militarytimes.com/off-duty/gearscout/irons/2019/10/05/this-was-the-gun-delta-operators-favored-during-the-battle-of-mogadishu)). One Delta sniper chose a 7.62 mm **M14 with an Aimpoint and an AIM-1 IR laser** over a long scope, which tells you how short the ranges were ([Forgotten Weapons](https://www.forgottenweapons.com/black-hawk-down-randy-shugharts-m14/)). The force also had shotguns, M203s, flashbangs, door charges, C-4 for blowing holes in walls, and IR strobes to mark friendly rooftops ([Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf); [Defense Media Network](https://defensemedianetwork.com/stories/task-force-ranger-20th-anniversary-the-battle-of-mogadishu-oct-3-1993/3)). The Rangers went light. They left night-vision goggles, canteens and bayonets behind and carried extra ammo and grenades instead, so after dark they had **few NVGs and no water** ([Bowden](https://archive.org/stream/black-hawk-down_201701/black-hawk-down_djvu.txt)). The new Ranger Body Armor is credited with saving at least 12 lives ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/212068/nsrdec_developed_body_armor_saved_lives_in_the_battle_of_mogadishu)). None of the sources documented uniforms or helmets. The DCU and PASGT gear seen in most depictions is plausible but unverified here.

**Aircraft and vehicles.** TFR had **16 helicopters**:

- **MH-60 Black Hawks**, callsign "Super."
- **MH-6** assault Little Birds and **AH-6** gunships, callsigns "Star" and "Barber." On this raid the AH-6s carried rockets for the first time.
- **Surveillance aircraft:** an OH-58D and two unmarked 500-series helicopters fitted with sensor balls that sent video down to the operations center ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/28177/these-secret-helicopters-were-flown-by-a-shadowy-unit-during-the-battle-of-mogadishu.md)).

Overnight, eight AH-6s rotated through two at a time. They fired about **70 rockets and tens of thousands of minigun rounds**, some of it "danger close" at about 10 yards from friendly troops ([Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)). The 10th Mountain QRF also had AH-1 Cobras and OH-58s.

The ground extraction column was **9 HMMWVs and 3 M939 5-ton trucks**. Some HMMWVs were Kevlar-armored and some were unarmored, and the trucks were flatbeds. The only real armor came from the UN: **Pakistani M48 tanks and Malaysian Condor APCs** (24 to 28 of them, depending on the source). TFR had asked in September for M1s, Bradleys and AC-130s, and Defense Secretary Aspin turned the request down ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/urban-case-study-9-the-battle-of-mogadishu/)).

**The Somali National Alliance (SNA).** Aidid's militia was mainly Habr Gidr clansmen, joined by Al-Itihaad elements and large numbers of untrained volunteers. Estimates of their strength vary widely. Pre-battle US intelligence counted about 300 loyal fighters, Biddle estimates about 1,500 citywide, and MWI says 2,000-4,000 converged on the raid ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/urban-case-study-9-the-battle-of-mogadishu/)). Most fighters carried AK-47s. Experienced men had RPG-7s, machine guns, sniper rifles, mortars and mines. Technicals mounted .50-cal/12.7 mm guns, and earlier fighting in 1993 had seen 106 mm recoilless rifles. Six 60 mm mortars were set up on October 3 but never fired ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)). No source confirms PKMs or ZU-23s on October 3.

## Smoke, crowds and rockets fired from behind

The SNA's tactics were improvised, but they worked together as a system:

- **Burning tires.** They signaled trouble and called fighters in, and burning-tire barricades of rubble went up at **nearly every intersection**. The barricades channeled convoys into ambushes ([Bowden](https://archive.org/stream/black-hawk-down_201701/black-hawk-down_djvu.txt); [MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/urban-case-study-9-the-battle-of-mogadishu/)).
- **Communications.** Cell phones called in more fighters. Commanders had dropped radios for couriers because the US was intercepting radio traffic. Men with megaphones urged residents to defend their homes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))).
- **Fighting style.** Groups of a dozen or more moved alley to alley, ducked out to fire, and stuck rifles around corners to fire without looking. Rooftop snipers fired from concealment and often missed ([Bowden](https://archive.org/stream/black-hawk-down_201701/black-hawk-down_djvu.txt); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)).
- **Civilians in the crowds.** Fighters mixed with unarmed women and children. Rangers could fire only at people pointing weapons at them. MWI cites a woman firing while holding a baby. Accounts disagree on how deliberate the use of human shields was ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/urban-case-study-9-the-battle-of-mogadishu/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))).
- **Changes through the night.** Fire became less frequent but more accurate as casual volunteers drifted home ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))). Bowden notes that khat chewing starts around noon, which put the raid in the militia's most wired and jumpy window.

**RPGs against helicopters** were the decisive weapon. RPG-7s downed Super 6-1 and Super 6-4. They forced Super 6-2 to crash-land at the New Port, nearly cut through Super 6-8's rotor blades, and badly damaged the Super 6-6 resupply bird ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)). The RPG-7 is normally fired from within about 45 m, but a skilled gunner can hit a stationary target out to about 1,600 ft ([USAF Museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/197664/rocket-propelled-grenades-old-threat-new-danger/)). That reach made helicopters hovering low and slow for fast-ropes, sniper cover and danger-close fire very vulnerable. Some sources claim Afghan-war veterans taught the militia to swap in timed fuzes, aim at tail rotors, fire from behind after a helicopter passes, and hide in pits. That sourcing is weak and rests largely on bin Laden's own boasts ([We Are The Mighty](https://www.wearethemighty.com/articles/osama-bin-laden-actually-had-a-hand-in-the-black-hawk-down-fiasco/)). Treat these as optional flavor, not established fact.

**The relief efforts.** The relief convoy was the third attempt to reach the trapped men. The first was the lost Ranger convoy. The second was the 10th Mountain QRF, ambushed on Via Lenin and stopped at K-4. A small Ranger column also lost two Humvees within about 1 km of base. All of these were back by about 17:45 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))). The night column formed at the New Port from 21:00. It was about 100 vehicles long and stretched about 1.4-2 miles. Pakistani M48s led it, Malaysian Condors carried 10th Mountain infantry, and HMMWVs (some with Mk19s) and trucks followed ([Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))). It drove north to National Street, where C Company turned toward the southern site and A Company toward the northern one. A wrong turn by the lead driver sent two Malaysian APCs south off National Street into an ambush ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/113311/we_didnt_leave_anybody_behind_10th_mountain_division_veterans_reflect_on_mogadishu_rescue_missi); [Seattle Times / Bowden](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19980204/2732545/black-hawk-down----an-american-war-story----rescue-convoy-in-confusion----chaos-reigns-as-commanders-assemble-multinational-rescue-effort)). Malaysian drivers refused several times to keep moving. A Company got out about five blocks from the hotel, and the column turned north on Shalalawi Street past the hotel, breaking through at 01:55 ([CMH](https://universityofleeds.github.io/philtaylorpapers/pmt/exhibits/2181/Somaliaarmy.pdf); [Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)). It then waited more than three hours while crews freed the pilot's body from the wreck. The vehicles were full, so the last men ran south down Shalalawi to National Street. This "Mogadishu Mile" was really about **400-600 m** by MWI's measure ([Wikipedia: Mogadishu Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu_Mile)). The movie version, in which the run ends at the stadium, is dramatized.

**The city.** In 1993 Mogadishu had over a million people after two years of civil war. Most buildings were **one or two stories of mud, adobe or stone**, and walls like that stopped little small-arms fire. A few buildings, the Olympic Hotel among them, rose four or more stories. White-towered mosques still stood among rubble and bullet-pocked walls ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)). The street plan was mostly a grid. Side streets were dirt and sand. Main roads were paved but cracked and littered with burned-out vehicles, and telephone poles leaned with their cable stripped. Displaced families lived in public buildings and in tin, wood and rag lean-tos, sometimes stacked on top of each other, which made many streets too narrow for vehicles ([MWI](https://mwi.westpoint.edu/?p=23172)). The Black Sea district was "tin-shanty hovels" behind 10-foot stone alley walls and courtyards. The area around crash site 1 was described as better-off, "middle-class" ([Marion](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Airmen-at-War/Marion-SpecialTacticsMogadishu1993.pdf)). At night the city was very dark because the power substations had been destroyed, and burning trucks and tire fires lit the streets ([Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/113311/we_didnt_leave_anybody_behind_10th_mountain_division_veterans_reflect_on_mogadishu_rescue_missi)). No source gives exact street widths or confirms flat roofs, though rooftop snipers and strobes imply people could get up on them.

## Turning the record into a game

**Map scale.** Build two layers. The **downtown core** should be about 1.5 x 1.5 km at true scale with 60-80 m blocks. It holds the hotel, the target, both crash sites, National Street and the route of the Mile, and should be dense, walled and enterable. The **operational map** should be about 6 x 6 km and can be sparser. Put the airport and hangar in the southwest, the New Port on the coast east of the airfield, K-4 between the airport and the southern crash site, the stadium in the northeast and the university/embassy compound on the far side. Place Super 6-4 between 600 m and 1.2 km south-southwest of Super 6-1. Make most alleys too narrow for vehicles. That one rule recreates both the lost convoy and the confusion of the night column.

**Mission list.** These follow the real order, so difficulty rises with the history:

| # | Mission | Main mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hangar mortar attack (Aug 28) | Night base defense |
| 2 | 03:00 compound raid (Aug 30) | Room clearing. The twist is that the detainees are UN staff. |
| 3-5 | Compound, police station, radio station and garage raids (Sep) | Fictional specifics, rising SNA awareness |
| 6 | Moving-car takedown (Sep 21) | Beacon tracking, helicopter sniping, first massed RPGs |
| 7 | Downed night patrol bird (Sep 25) | Evade with a pistol for about 2 hours, or hold the wreck as QRF |
| 8 | Fast-rope and blocking positions (Oct 3) | Brownout misdrop, fight one block back to the corner |
| 9 | Little Bird street pickup / CSAR rope-in | Hold a hover under RPG fire, treat casualties, blow holes in walls |
| 10 | The lost convoy | Driving under fire with bad directions, prisoners aboard, abort |
| 11 | Via Lenin ambush / K-4 | Ammo-heavy ambush you must break contact from |
| 12 | Two-man sniper holdout at crash site 2 | Last stand with limited ammo and no rescue (scripted loss) |
| 13 | Overnight perimeter | No NVGs, civilians in the houses, resupply drop, strobe-marked gun runs |
| 14 | Night armored relief | Escort a 100-vehicle column, rescue the lost APCs, Bakara Market |
| 15 | Hold for recovery | Defend the wreck about 3 hours while the body is cut free |
| 16 | The Mogadishu Mile | Dawn movement on foot with air cover to the rendezvous and stadium |

**Enemy AI.** Base the militia's behavior on what the record shows:

- **Escalating alarm.** Burning-tire smoke columns act as beacons that pull reinforcement waves toward the player. The enemy count starts near 300 and grows into the thousands over the afternoon.
- **Loose cells.** Groups of about a dozen move alley to alley, and their fire is high-volume and low-accuracy, including blind fire around corners.
- **Accuracy over time.** Fewer fighters but better aim after dark.
- **Rare RPG specialists.** They wait for low, slow helicopters or convoys halted at roadblocks, and prefer to fire from cover or from behind.
- **Technicals.** Used as mobile heavy machine guns.
- **Roadblocks.** Hasty barricades go up across the routes the player has used.
- **Civilians mixed into crowds.** Back this with a rules-of-engagement mechanic that penalizes firing on unarmed people. Add an enemy commander who holds back mortars when civilians are nearby.

**Weapon and vehicle roster.**

- **US weapons:** CAR-15 (Colt 723) with a red dot, M16, M203, SAW, M60, M14 with red dot and IR laser, a pump shotgun (including one mounted under a carbine), M9 pistol, flashbangs, door charges and C-4.
- **US support tools:** IR strobes and radio calls for AH-6 gun runs.
- **SNA weapons:** AK-47, RPG-7, technical-mounted 12.7 mm gun, scoped rifles, 60 mm mortars and 106 mm recoilless rifles.
- **Vehicles:** unarmored and Kevlar-armored HMMWVs, M939 5-ton trucks, HMMWVs with Mk19s, Condor APCs, M48 tanks, MH-6 and AH-6 Little Birds, MH-60 Black Hawks, and a surveillance-camera aircraft used as an intel overlay.
- **Helicopter damage states:** run from cosmetic damage, to limping home (like Super 6-8), to a controlled crash-landing (like Super 6-2), to a full crash (like Super 6-1 and 6-4).
- **Leave out M1s, Bradleys and AC-130s.** They were requested and refused, and their absence is part of the story.

**Use fictional characters.** Build the cast from roles, not real people: a Delta assaulter, a Delta sniper pair, a Ranger chalk leader and M60 gunner, an Air Force pararescueman and combat controller, an MH-6 pilot pair, a 10th Mountain squad leader, a Malaysian APC driver and a Pakistani tank commander. Many of the men in these events were killed, captured or decorated, and their families and fellow soldiers are alive today. Two of the dead received posthumous Medals of Honor, and the captured pilot has a public identity ([Wikipedia Timeline](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Timeline_of_the_1993_Battle_of_Mogadishu)). Putting real names into a game where the player decides who lives or dies invites justified offense and possible legal trouble. Fictional names keep the units, callsigns and events accurate and free you to write dialogue and outcomes. A dedication or end-credits note can still honor the real people.

## Conclusion

The documented record contradicts much of the famous "Black Hawk Down" picture in ways a game can use. The Mile was a few hundred meters, not a mile. The relief column spent longer waiting at the wreck than fighting its way in. The decisive enemy weapon was a cheap rocket aimed at aircraft that had to hover to do their jobs. Many US problems were self-inflicted: NVGs left behind, armor that was requested and refused, and a command helicopter that could see the convoy but could not guide it through the alleys. A semi-realistic design gets its tension from those constraints, not from a larger enemy body count.

The biggest open questions are spatial. The position of crash site 2, the exact turn-by-turn route of the lost convoy, and coordinates for K-4, the stadium and the New Port are all uncertain or missing in these sources. Before building final geometry, check them against Bowden's full text, the CMH study, the 1995 Senate review and period satellite imagery. Until then, keep those locations in the approximate ranges given above.
